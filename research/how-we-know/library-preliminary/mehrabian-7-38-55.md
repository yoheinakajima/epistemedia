## Circulated proposition versus tested propositions

The circulated proposition is that “93% of communication is nonverbal,” commonly divided into
7% words, 38% tone, and 55% body language or facial expression.

The 1967 experiments instead measured how undergraduates inferred positive, neutral, or
negative attitude toward an addressee:

1. one-word semantic content crossed with vocal tone; and
2. the neutral word “maybe” in three tones, paired with head-only facial photographs.

They did not measure all communication, natural conversation, factual comprehension,
persuasion, memory, gestures, posture, dress, spatial behavior, or three simultaneously varied
verbal/vocal/facial channels in a single experiment.

## Cross-study derivation audit

P2's final paragraph combines its implications with P1 and **suggests** coefficients
`.07`, `.38`, and `.55` for simultaneous verbal, vocal, and facial attitude communication. It
calls the step an extrapolation to be tested. Neither experiment manipulated all three channels.

- `1.50 / 1.03 = 1.4563`, close to `.55 / .38 = 1.4474`.
- Reserving `.07` and allocating the remaining `.93` in P2's `1.50:1.03` ratio produces
  `.5514` and `.3786`.
- That arithmetic is a reconstruction, not a derivation printed in either paper.
- The origin of the initial `.07` is not disclosed in P1 or P2 and cannot be recovered from
  P1's reported ANOVA as a stated verbal weight.

The formula's appearance in P2 is verified; the complete derivation is unresolved.
